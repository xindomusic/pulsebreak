# Resonance: color, drum and bass, and combat impact

Date: 2026-09-13. Baseline: `dd5608f`. This research responds to the player's report that the colors feel bland, the music needs more energy, and battle/blast feedback should feel cooler. The player's subsequent choice is **drum and bass**. Recommendations therefore target a 174 BPM broken-beat arrangement, not the earlier generic four-on-the-floor EDM suggestion. Earlier research and reviews remain historical.

## Baseline diagnosis

The existing `tools/generate_audio.py` composes 16 bars at 112 BPM in two mono stems. Its foundation uses sustained sine-based pads, a bell-like eighth-note arpeggio and a repeating four-region chord progression. Kick, snare and hats all live in the pressure stem. At low intensity, the runtime reduces that entire stem to −34 dB before the user's volume gain. These are source observations, not listening judgments. They explain why changing only tempo or master volume would leave the composition's soft instrumentation and limited arrangement contrast intact.

The existing visuals already separate four weapon colors and have functional impact/death effects. The new task is to create more persuasive contrast and event emphasis while preserving warning readability. A higher particle count or brighter entire frame cannot establish that improvement.

## Four concrete decisions

1. **Compose a rolling drum-and-bass track with an actual arc.** Use a 174 BPM, 64-bar arrangement, approximately 88.28 seconds. Write a recognizable syncopated kick pattern, sharp backbeats, quieter ghost hits and varied hats/shuffles. Separate centered sub weight from detuned, filtered mid-bass character; give the lead a changing phrase rather than indefinitely repeating the old bell pattern. A local arrangement candidate is 8 bars of groove introduction, 16 of first drop, 8 of development, 8 of break/build, 16 of varied second drop, and 8 of transition back to the loop. Musical content and density should change at section boundaries. This is a local composition proposal, not a rule prescribed by the sources.
2. **Make the track respond to battle without losing its groove.** Keep an audible rhythmic foundation, add drive from nearby threats/incoming shots, and reveal lead or upper percussion during high energy and Guardian combat. Keep all stems on the same sample-length timeline; change gains without restarting the song. Use short attack and slower release for intensity so one enemy crossing a distance threshold does not repeatedly toggle the mix. Reserve music ducking for meaningful events; routine automatic fire should not suppress the track continuously.
3. **Make a pulse read as a decisive event.** Give discharge a clear origin, a very short bright core, then a thin expanding boundary and fading fragments. Keep the apparent footprint consistent with actual damage. Pair it with a defined transient, low-frequency body and shorter high-frequency tail. Avoid opaque full-arena flashes that hide the next attack. Charge strength may change scale/timbre, but must not delay the actual input or damage until a musical beat. In the palette, place saturated warm/cool accents against controlled darker surfaces; keep the player, objective and hostile warning distinguishable at the gameplay camera.
4. **Deliver evidence a player can actually judge.** Supply a playable music preview with an entire arrangement cycle and a second preview using representative live gains, combat cues and ducking. Label offline reconstruction separately from captured engine output. Compare matching normal-camera Full/Low Effects scenes and timed discharge frames, then measure one complete native M4 run with no competing engine. Numeric WAV checks, screenshots and a score cannot substitute for listening or human play.

## Practical Mac mini M4 evaluation

Record the actual model/chip, memory, OS, engine/renderer, window pixels, captured image dimensions, effects/shadow settings and music volume. Run one native game instance at the target settings through ordinary fights, a crowded blast, a Guardian and generated travel. Keep loading/transition timing distinct from active combat. Compare sustained median/tail intervals and object bounds; if a visible hitch is repeatable, correlate it with the event before changing the renderer.

The earlier 16.7 ms p95 is an **optimization goal**, not an automatic ceiling on a quality rating. An 18 ms tail around a stable 60 Hz presentation can have a different cause and player impact from prolonged stalls. Conversely, a good median cannot excuse a repeated freeze during blasts. The target is stable, responsive play on this machine, and every claim must match the actual measurement. Headless or fixed-step policy runs do not measure graphics performance.

The review will separately record composition/asset correctness, engine mix behavior, actual listening and player acceptance. [Resonance review](../qa/resonance-review.md) contains the rubric and final evidence when assembled.

## Audition feedback: stronger bass and drums

After auditioning a preview, the player explicitly requested more aggressive bass and drums. This is useful listening feedback and a revision request, not acceptance of the first mix. The next candidate should strengthen snare body and attack, kick definition, and the moving upper harmonics of the Reese bass while reducing the lead's relative prominence. Preserve a centered sub and a short kick-clearance envelope; evaluate the combined result before raising the entire output.

Native Instruments' [Reese bass walkthrough](https://blog.native-instruments.com/reese-bass/) explains the moving harmonics produced by detuned oscillators and demonstrates overdrive and evolving filtering. Its [distortion production guide](https://blog.native-instruments.com/distortion-in-music/) describes adding bass harmonics and blending saturated/compressed drums with their dry signal. These support changing timbre and rhythmic density, with parallel processing retaining some original transients. They do not prescribe the exact gains or prove that this game's synthesized revision is satisfying. Both written guides were read; their embedded sound examples were not auditioned by the reviewer.
